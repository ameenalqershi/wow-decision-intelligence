# Independent mathematical review

**Reviewed 28 November 2026.** This derivation and finite checker were prepared by a reviewer separate from the principal author. The review concerns the assertions below. “Mathematically Proven” refers to the stated theorem under its premises. A running adapter satisfies those premises only to the extent separately established by its qualification evidence.

## 1. Objects and exact transformations

For a revision r let Fᵣ be its set of source-feasible decisions and fᵣ its scalar cost to minimize. Let Gᵣ be the feasible decisions of a compiled model and gᵣ its cost. Use exact arithmetic in a common ordered number system throughout this appendix. Let p map compiled decisions back to source decisions.

**Theorem 1 — semantic reconstruction (Mathematically Proven).** Suppose p maps Gᵣ onto Fᵣ and for every y in Gᵣ the equality gᵣ(y) = fᵣ(p(y)) holds. Then the sets of attainable objective values agree. If y attains a compiled minimum, p(y) attains a source minimum. Either both feasible sets are empty or both are nonempty.

*Proof.* Every compiled value appears in the source by the value identity. For a source value at x, surjectivity provides a preimage y with p(y)=x, and the value identity supplies the reverse inclusion. The claims about extrema and emptiness follow from equality of the value sets. Auxiliary-variable lifts can have many preimages.

*Failure witness.* Fix x=0 without justification in min −x over x∈{0,1}. The retained decision is feasible, reconstructs correctly, and has cost 0. The true minimum is −1. Independent verification of the returned assignment establishes feasibility and cost only. For an offset k recorded during compilation, compare gᵣ(y)+k to the original cost on both sides, including in all reported bounds.

## 2. Complete factor scopes

Let V be a finite index set of variables, each with an explicit domain. Let the blocks B₁,…,Bₖ form a disjoint cover of V. Treat a resource capacity, global budget, preference, time conversion and scenario equality as a factor if it affects admissibility or value. Evaluate variable-free constraints before splitting. An empty conjunction is true. Retain global scalar offset c exactly once.

**Theorem 2 — product optimum (Mathematically Proven).** Suppose F equals the Cartesian product of feasible block sets Fᵢ. Suppose f(x)=c+Σᵢ fᵢ(xᵢ), where xᵢ is the projection onto Bᵢ. If each Fᵢ is nonempty and fᵢ attains its minimum mᵢ, the combined local minimizers attain the global minimum c+Σᵢmᵢ. One empty block implies global infeasibility. For zero blocks, after checking constants the unique empty assignment costs c.

*Proof.* Membership in the product is equivalent to membership of all projections. Each local optimality inequality fᵢ(xᵢ)≥mᵢ holds simultaneously, so the sum is a global lower bound. The tuple of local minimizers belongs to the product and attains the sum. An empty factor admits no tuple. This argument does not require uniqueness. An infeasible factor takes precedence over any unbounded feasible factor when reporting global status.

*Certificate construction.* Each active row and each summand records all indices it reads after expanding definitions. A membership check confirms each factor's support belongs to one block. Together with product domains these checks suffice to establish the theorem's premises. An opaque term with unknown support prevents acceptance. Variable-free false constraints cannot disappear because no incidence vertex reaches them. A common immutable parameter can influence multiple block proofs when it changes across revisions while introducing no decision coupling in a single fixed revision.

*Failure witnesses.* Minimize −x−y for two binary independent decisions. Both local minima set their decision to 1. Adding x+y≤1 makes (1,1) infeasible and changes the optimum from −2 to −1. Keeping the domains and adding 3xy to the cost makes (1,1) feasible but suboptimal, at 1 versus −1. Removing x=y can expand the feasible set even though the new graph contains no trace of that old edge. A scenario's shared first-stage variable has one stable identity across scenarios. Cloning it into independent decisions relaxes nonanticipativity.

## 3. Transport across revisions

Align variable identities, units, objective direction, comparison semantics and offsets before applying any inequality. A lower bound L for old minimization means f(x)≥L for every x in F. Let F′ be the revised feasible set, f′ the revised cost, and δ(x)=f′(x)−f(x) wherever both are defined.

**Theorem 3 — restricted transport (Mathematically Proven).** If F′⊆F and δ(x)≥b over F′, then L+b is a valid revised lower bound. A currently feasible x̂ with f′(x̂)=L+b establishes optimality. A strict gap only gives a valid lower bound and an incumbent upper bound.

*Proof.* For x in F′, set inclusion licenses f(x)≥L and the residual guard gives δ(x)≥b. Addition yields f′(x)≥L+b. Equality at a feasible point attains the bound. The proof allows F′ to be empty, but an empty set cannot produce the feasible witness required for the optimality assertion.

**Corollary for binary linear changes.** Let dⱼ be the change in coefficient j, Δc the global offset change, and xⱼ∈{0,1}. Then b=Δc+Σⱼmin(0,dⱼ) is a valid relaxation bound. More generally, for guaranteed finite intervals [lⱼ,uⱼ], replace each summand by min(dⱼlⱼ,dⱼuⱼ). The independent interval minima may be mutually unattainable. Equality is sufficient, sometimes inconclusive. Example: with x+y=1 and f=x+3y, the optimum is 1. Adding 2x+2y changes every feasible value by 2. The box residual gives 0 and reports lower bound 1; the true revised optimum is 3, attained by the old minimizer.

**Certificate variant excluding the incumbent.** If a separately verified lower bound M covers every old feasible decision *other than* x̂, then for surviving x̂ compare f′(x̂)≤M+b. This suffices even when f′(x̂) exceeds a transported lower bound derived from the old optimum. A bound on the next *distinct objective value* fails in the presence of ties. Example: both binary choices formerly cost 0; an update of −1 at the other choice makes it strictly cheaper. Exact second-best evidence must quantify over all competing assignments. This is an alternative proof obligation, independent of which strategy is implemented.

*Counterexamples.* Expansion from F={1} to F′={0,1}, minimizing x, invalidates the old lower bound 1. For an unchanged binary domain, f=0 followed by f′=−2x invalidates lower bound 0 without correction. Converting decimal USD values to cents multiplies values by 100; applying an additive residual in the old scale to a new-scale bound has no mathematical justification. For a maximization claim, apply the argument to the negated objective, then convert direction and infinity flags back.

## 4. Durable reuse, bounded repair and termination

**Theorem 4 — identity transfer (Mathematically Proven under validation premises).** An authenticated local proof of proposition P about canonical mathematical payload A can be used for B if the validator checks complete payload equality and identical proof interpretation. A digest match proposes candidates for validation. The equality check compares canonical bytes under an unambiguous schema, including domain, variable map, constants, unit/time semantics and objective semantics. Runtime trust rules check tenant, source provenance and checker version separately. An untrusted store supplying a matching payload and fabricated proof must still fail proof replay or protected-record authentication.

**Theorem 5 — repair (Mathematically Proven under Theorem 2).** If a revised model has a verified product partition, all reused local minima are proved for the applicable revised blocks, and every remaining block is solved exactly, composition is globally optimal by Theorem 2. A feasible repair with an arbitrary frozen boundary proves only a restricted optimum. For example under x=y with f=−x−y, freezing x=0 permits only (0,0); the global optimum is (1,1). If solving a local block stops early, its feasible solution can participate in a global incumbent only after complete reconstruction and checking. Exact global status requires an independent closing bound.

**Termination and fallback (Verified Within Explicit Limits).** Finite exhaustive enumeration terminates with exact arithmetic when every evaluation terminates. A bounded coordinator requires monotone consumed budget, finite retries and wall deadlines on external tasks, including proof generation, I/O, cancellation and cleanup. Forced worker isolation or an enforceable shutdown deadline is needed for a hung native process. Fallback discards invalid restrictions, builds the entire revised model, then applies the same evidence gate as on the cold path. Fallback can return unknown, feasible-only or timeout; mathematical optimality follows from an accepted proof, equally for partial and full solving. Infinite retries against stale revisions do not satisfy the bounded protocol.

**Concurrency counterexample.** A verifier proves revision 9, then a writer publishes revision 10 between the version check and pointer update. Publishing the result to a mutable “current” pointer without an atomic revision comparison breaks binding. Commit the checked revision, evidence digest and result together with a compare-and-swap on expected revision, or publish to an immutable historical record. Stale retries consume the original request budget.

## 5. Independent finite replay

Run `python3 check_transport.py` from this directory and compare its JSON with [finite-audit.json](finite-audit.json). The program uses exact integers and a truth table independent of any C# implementation. It enumerates all old nonempty feasible subsets of the two-bit cube, all their nonempty revised subsets, and every old coefficient vector and change in {−1,0,1}². There are 65 nested nonempty subset pairs and 81 coefficient-vector pairs, giving 5,265 lower-bound checks. Of these, 3,308 bounds are strict. The 2,128 feasible equality witnesses include ties. Separately all 16 pairs of feasible subsets on two single-bit components are crossed with 27 objective/offset choices, giving 432 product cases including empty factors.

**Decision.** Theorems 1–3 are proved within their hypotheses. The independent enumeration corroborates the stated finite instances. Runtime benefit, numerical correctness of external adapters, completeness of the production dependency schema, and production concurrency remain separate claims.

## 6. Implemented disjoint-pair gate

The auditor inspected `research/AlgorithmDiscoveryLab/Candidates.cs` and `Corpus.cs`. The generator fixes binary variable identities and uses ordered unary integer prices. The inclusion test requires each old capacity record to occur in the new array. This syntactic test implies feasibility containment for this grammar and may reject semantically valid tightenings written differently. Exception and arithmetic behavior outside the generated range require separate guards before general admission.

For an exactly-one row on indices i and j, the two possible residual values are dᵢ and dⱼ. Its exact pair floor is min(dᵢ,dⱼ). Select any collection of variable-disjoint eligible pairs; add their pair floors once and the ordinary single-coordinate floors on all remaining variables. Every current feasible assignment satisfies each selected row, so its residual is at least the sum. Extra constraints only reduce the feasible set further. The greedy order changes the bound strength and runtime but cannot compromise this inequality. The floor dominates the ordinary box floor termwise. For both dᵢ,dⱼ≥0 the difference is the smaller coefficient; for both negative it is the magnitude of the less negative coefficient; for opposite signs it is zero. For a negative example of double counting, use x+y=1 and y+z=1 with all coefficient changes +1. Summing both pair floors gives 2 although (0,1,0) incurs only 1.

The candidate checks that the previous optimum remains feasible and recomputes its new cost. Accepting it exactly when its new cost equals the transported lower bound is therefore sound for this model grammar and tested integer range, provided the previous optimum is correct. The initial result comes from exhaustive local solving. Induction applies to later accepted transfers. Costs stay safely below the 32-bit range on the frozen synthetic generators. Extending input sizes or coefficient magnitudes requires checked arithmetic or wider exact types.

An independently written truth-table script checks all ordered lists of distinct candidate pairs on three bits (including the triangle with no feasible point) against every change vector in {−2,−1,0,1,2}³. Run `python3 check_pair_floor.py` and compare [pair-audit.json](pair-audit.json). This test exercises overlapping rows and conflicting constraints absent from many simple manufacturing examples. It verifies the mathematical floor separately from end-to-end solver verification.

## 7. A stronger challenger for a future generation

**Proposed Hypothesis for net benefit.** Overlapping exactly-one pair constraints define a graph. Within each connected component, fix a reference bit to 0 and propagate alternating bits along its edges. A parity conflict proves infeasibility because following an odd cycle would require a bit to equal its complement. Otherwise the two assignments obtained by flipping all component bits exhaust the assignments satisfying those pair relations. This follows by induction along any spanning tree, followed by checking the remaining edges. For isolated vertices these are the two unary choices.

For each consistent component sum the residual coefficient changes on the two alternating assignments and take the smaller sum. Summing these minima is the exact residual minimum over the relaxation containing all eligible exactly-one pair rows. The feasible set with every such row is contained in the relaxation retaining any disjoint subset, so its bound dominates the greedy gate. Traverse adjacency lists once in O(n+e) graph and arithmetic operations with e pair rows, after reading and validating the model. Bit complexity depends on the sizes of summed coefficients. Additional hard constraints can still leave a loose floor. Prematurely declaring feasibility on the basis of pair consistency alone would ignore those constraints.

For x+y=y+z=1 and d=(1,4,1), the residual values are 4 at (0,1,0) and 2 at (1,0,1). The exact pair-graph floor is 2. The greedy disjoint floor is 1. On disjoint pairs the two gates agree. A separate parity-graph arm and an independent enumeration check should be frozen before a new holdout is drawn. Construction overhead and scarcity of overlapping pair rows could erase any runtime advantage. This is ordinary bipartite consistency and relaxation applied to artifact transport. It makes no novelty or measured advantage claim.
