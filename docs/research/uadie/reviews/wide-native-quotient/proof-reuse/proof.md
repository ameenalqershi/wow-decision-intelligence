# G16 exact structural proof reuse
Let S=(n,E,F,ordered row scopes and bounds) and c be the unary objective. The G15 transformation theorem establishes a bijection between source feasible assignments and reduced feasible assignments via x_i=b_i+(1-2b_i)y_j (or fixed b_i), with every source row transformed exactly. This claim depends on S, not c. Certify the same S with zero objective, independently checking the graph, fixed anchors, map, and every transformed row. Hold a private copy of S and the verified certificate, with no external aliases. A new S' is eligible for reuse iff exact ordered content equality S'=S; SHA256 is only evidence binding, not the admission test. Consequently the identical feasible-set equivalence applies without repeating structural verification. No partial-update claim: changed edges (add/remove/merge/split), fixes, row bounds/memberships or order rebuild. Objective-only changes do not affect structural proof. For current c', form offset=sum c'_i b_i and coefficient d_j=sum c'_i(1-2b_i). Independent Int128 verifier uses fixed contribution plus per-component orientation costs C0,C1, checking offset=sum C0+fixed and d_j=C1-C0. Thus current cost is exact, regardless of old cost. Full result certificate is deep-copied for evidence; mutation cannot alter token. Fresh reduced solve or direct exchange proof, original witness and reconstruction verify current solution. No old optimum or bound assumed. Contradictory graph/fix abstains to original native solve. Native optimality remains solver status, not independent formal proof.
Hit complexity O(n+e+f+q) exact identity plus O(n+q) copied certificate/objective/reconstruction/encoding; misses additionally repeat G15 graph/row proof. Memory includes private snapshot and proof, current copy and native state. Native search worst case unchanged. Single owner; inputs must not mutate during snapshot; no tenant authorization or external hostile serialization contract. Full persisted records include source model, current certificate, reduced bits, original bits and status. Readback reconstructs independently and validates all source constraints, exact cost, full transformation and direct proof where applicable. It does not verify general native optimality, authenticate revision metadata, detect missing whole records without external manifest, or prove crash durability.
